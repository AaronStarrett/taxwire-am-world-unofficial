# City edition: a working day with consequences

**Unofficial training prototype. Not endorsed by Taxwire. Fictional customers. Educational simulation—not tax advice.**

## Explore the city

The original eight addresses remain in the same places, so earlier saves and guided-first-day destinations still make sense. Their new towers contain real walkable upper floors, reached from the lift directory near each entrance. Floors have named work zones and furnished layouts for account work, conferences, source research, learning, studio work and quieter conversations. The larger bounded district adds streets, landscaping and places to explore.

- Walk with WASD or arrow keys, or the touch movement pad. Drag to rotate the camera; click a clear floor or pavement to walk there.
- Press **E** or tap the nearby interaction cue for a person, workstation, document or lift. Walk close enough to see its name.
- Use a lift to change floor. **R** recovers to a safe arrival on your current floor. The map directory also offers explicit travel to any listed floor.
- On phones, the first-day guide starts as a small **Guide** button. Tap it to expand, use **Hide** to collapse it, and reopen whenever needed. This browser remembers your presentation choice; collapsing never skips a step or erases progress.
- **M** opens the map, **J** the journal, **P** pauses and **Esc** closes a panel. Movement pauses while using work tools or typing.
- Low quality, reduced motion, text scaling and the accessible workbench remain in Settings. The workbench runs the same decisions, clock and saved history without WebGL.

The city uses original procedural architecture, surfaces and adult character models. These are detailed game assets, not photorealistic scans. Floors use a bounded family of furnished layouts, not a claim of a unique authored room or mission for every floor. Towers do not hide an infinite generated city.

## Meet people

Every displayed character has a contact identity. Approach them in the world, or use **People** to browse the full directory. A contact brief shows their role, remit, communication preferences and remembered commitments. You can also travel to their physical workplace from the conversation panel.

Choose a conversation topic:

1. **Discovery:** understand the business, ask for a precise example and agree what remains unknown.
2. **Coffee:** offer an optional professional conversation, respect a decline, schedule it and actually attend at Common Ground café.
3. **Repair:** acknowledge a problem, preserve the original record and agree a realistic corrective step.
4. **Scope:** distinguish evidence, authority and commercial pressure without inventing approval.
5. **Handoff:** bring in a specialist while retaining clear account ownership.

Replies change the next exchange and may lead to a constructive, limited, poor or recovered outcome. The debrief explains what worked, what needs improvement, the next commitment and the actual trust/risk effect. Contact and account relationship records persist. Replaying a topic remains possible but cannot farm additional relationship points; a separate repair conversation can establish a bounded corrective outcome.

A coffee invitation is not automatically accepted. Availability, timing, existing commitments and relationship state affect the response. Accepted meetings appear in the simulated calendar. Travel to the café and advance only the necessary business time before attending. A missed meeting has a different route from a meeting that was attended. No real invitation or external message is sent.

Closing a conversation preserves its current stage. Reopen the contact and choose **Resume this conversation**. An unfinished conversation must be resolved before changing that contact's topic.

## Work cases with real forks

All 52 established cases retain their identifiers, original evidence, competencies and strongest evidence-backed route. New attempts use a separately versioned decision graph:

- An evidence-backed action advances the work.
- A provisional decision can open a clarification, a bounded continuation or a pause with owned residual work.
- An unsupported action can provoke a challenge. A correction returns to the actual unresolved decision, rather than silently awarding a pass.
- Refusing to correct or making a critical breach can end the conversation poorly.
- Completing the stronger route after acknowledging a mistake produces a visible recovery history. It does not erase the original mistake or waive assessment requirements.

The sidebar shows the path actually taken. The final debrief distinguishes the business outcome from whether the evidence met the existing passing standard. A friendly interaction is not proof of a correct calculation, tax treatment, acceptance record or completed obligation.

Earlier in-progress attempts continue with their original linear contract. Earlier completed attempts and grades remain intact. Fresh attempts opt into the new branching contract. The first-day control tutorial remains a guided sequence; its skill practice leads into the branching case and contact systems.

## Saves and boundaries

Browser-local save version 3 and the existing portable formats remain in place. Separate versioned extensions preserve city-floor context and conversation sessions, history, appointments and relationship outcomes. Unrecognized newer versions are protected rather than silently overwritten. Floor recovery changes presentation only and does not reset case progress.

No new API, model service, paid asset, telemetry service, real CRM or external account integration is used. The existing free GitHub Pages workflow publishes only a validated production build. Localhost, the hosted site and other browsers remain separate save origins.

Qualified tax/legal review, employer procedure approval, human learning validation and universal device-performance guarantees remain outside this release's claims. See `KNOWN_GAPS.md` and `BUILD_STATE.md` for executed acceptance evidence and limitations.
